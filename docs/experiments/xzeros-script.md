# Video script: The zeros inside E8's stack

Module `xzeros` (the 25th in the Experiments menu, desktop and VR). The lines below are the narration as recorded in
[`experiments.json`](../../Assets/Resources/Narration/experiments.json); the module plays them with captions and its
camera and reveal cues, and it can be narrated with the tour's voice and recorded like the others
(`Tools/generate_narration.py --script experiments`, then `Tools/render_chapters.py --script experiments --shapes vertical`).
Sections JA–JF of the [experiments README](README.md) hold the numbers behind every line.

**Title card:** The zeros inside E8's stack — *where zeta's zeros live, and how E8 sorts the primes*

559 words, about 3.6 minutes at the narrator's pace.

## 1. where are ζ's zeros in the stack?

*On screen:* The factor funnel of E8's wheel, n ≤ 400, builds piece by piece in its prime colours. Slow orbit, low angle.

*Board:* **where are ζ's zeros in the stack?** — every n at its hour, its column as tall as log n

> Here is E eight's factor funnel again. Every number up to four hundred stands at its hour on the wheel, with a column as tall as its logarithm. So where, in this stack, are the zeros of zeta?

## 2. a zero is a coil up the axis

*On screen:* Close on the axis. The first zero's coil (gold) draws in from the floor to the top.

*Board:* **a zero is a coil up the axis** — n turns by γ·log n — its height times γ

> Not at any point. At a zero, one half plus i gamma, every number n is turned by gamma times log n: its height in the funnel, times gamma. So a zero is a coil wound up the axis. The first zero, fourteen point one three, turns once every point four four of height.

## 3. the first three zeros

*On screen:* Same close shot, turning. The second (blue) and third (pink) coils draw in inside the first.

*Board:* **the first three zeros** — γ = 14.13, 21.02 and 25.01

> The second and third zeros, twenty-one point zero two and twenty-five point zero one, are tighter coils. And every drop of a path back, dividing by a prime p, lowers the height by log p and turns every coil by gamma times log p.

## 4. at the height of 7, a prime

*On screen:* Looking down at the height of 7. A ring of 48 orange bars around the coils: where all 10 500 zeros point there. The white arrow (their mean) leans to −1.

*Board:* **at the height of 7, a prime** — 10 500 coils lean to −1: mean −0.115, Landau −0.115

> Now take all ten thousand five hundred zeros and look at one height: where seven sits. Their coils lean towards minus one. On average, by minus point one one five, which is exactly what Landau's formula predicts for a prime.

## 5. at the height of 6 = 2·3

*On screen:* The same at the height of 6: white bars, even all round; the arrow shrinks to nothing.

*Board:* **at the height of 6 = 2·3** — the coils cancel: mean 0.000

> At the height of six, two times three, the coils point every way, and their mean is zero. The zeros lean only at prime powers.

## 6. added up, the coils light every prime power

*On screen:* Side view. The coils go; the sum of the zeros grows out to the right, tooth by tooth from the floor up, each tooth ending on the funnel's profile at a prime. Labels 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 31, 97, 211, 397.

*Board:* **added up, the coils light every prime power** — a tooth of log p, reaching the top of p's column

> So add up all the coils at every height, together with zeta's pole, and draw the sum out to the side. What is left is a comb: a tooth of exactly log p at every prime power, reaching the top of that prime's column, and nothing at the composites.

## 7. spun round the axis: a ring per prime power

*On screen:* The camera rises while each tooth spins into a ring through its column: the rings stack into the funnel's bell.

*Board:* **spun round the axis: a ring per prime power** — ζ's zeros fix the height, not the hour

> But the comb is the same in every direction. Spin it round the axis, and each tooth becomes a ring through its prime's column. Zeta's zeros know how high a prime sits, but not its hour on the wheel.

## 8. E8's field has eight L-functions

*On screen:* Cut to the eight trees, two rows of four. Only ζ's tree grows: every tooth to the right.

*Board:* **E8's field has eight L-functions** — one per character of (Z/30)*, E8's exponents — ζ is the first

> E eight knows more. Its Coxeter element makes E eight a lattice over the thirtieth roots of unity, and their field has eight L-functions, one for each character of E eight's exponent group. Zeta is the first.

## 9. each turns the primes by χ(p)

*On screen:* The other seven grow in turn. Real characters send teeth both ways, the quartic ones four ways; same colour (hour), same direction.

*Board:* **each turns the primes by χ(p)** — ±1 for the real characters, ±1 and ±i for the rest

> The zeros of each one light the same prime powers, but turned by the character: back and forth for the real ones, and all four ways for the others. Every tooth of one colour, one hour, points the same way.

## 10. turned back by χ(h) and added

*On screen:* Back to the funnel, from above. The eight fans appear one by one as their hours are named, each ray ending on a prime's column.

*Board:* **turned back by χ(h) and added** — only the primes on spoke h survive

> Now turn each set of zeros back by its character at hour h, and add all eight. Only the primes on spoke h survive. Spoke one. Seven. Eleven. Thirteen. Seventeen. Nineteen. Twenty-three. Twenty-nine.

## 11. 7 420 zeros up to t = 1000

*On screen:* All eight fans; the camera climbs and turns.

*Board:* **7 420 zeros up to t = 1000** — ζ's give the height, the other seven the hour

> Seven thousand four hundred zeros, none higher than a thousand, sort every prime up to four hundred onto its spoke. Zeta's zeros give the height. The other seven give the hour.

## 12. every drop is a lamp the zeros light

*On screen:* Cut to the paths chart: the staircases of 360, 343 and 397 appear one after the other, each drop with its lamp beside it and the zeros' sum under each stop.

*Board:* **every drop is a lamp the zeros light** — log n = Σ Λ(d) over the prime powers d dividing n

> And the paths back walk through these lamps. Three sixty drops log two three times, log three twice, and log five once. Each drop is exactly the sum of the zeros at a prime power dividing it: two cubed, two squared, two, three squared, three and five. At the stops in between, the zeros cancel.

## 13. within one L-function: repulsion

*On screen:* The spacing charts: pair correlation within ζ against 1 − (sin πu/πu)², the flat correlation between L-functions, the gaps.

*Board:* **within one L-function: repulsion** — between the eight: none — eight independent spectra

> Among themselves, the zeros of each L-function repel, like the eigenvalues of a random unitary matrix. Zeros of different L-functions ignore each other. E eight's field holds eight independent spectra.

## 14. the zeros are frequencies of the height

*On screen:* Back to the funnel with coils, comb and fans together; a wide slow orbit to the end.

*Board:* **the zeros are frequencies of the height** — they rebuild the primes — they do not predict them

> So the zeros are not points in the stack. They are frequencies of its height. Added up, they rebuild the prime powers. Turned by E eight's characters, they put each prime on its spoke. They reconstruct the primes, but they do not predict them.
